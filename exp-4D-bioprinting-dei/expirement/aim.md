### Aim of the Experiment
To model the full extrusion-bioprinting workflow for 4D tissue constructs, spanning five coupled rheological, kinetic, biological, and mechanical problems that decide whether a printed scaffold ever functions:
1. **Bioink Printability:** Characterize a shear-thinning bioink with the power-law model &eta;(&gamma;&#775;) = K&middot;&gamma;&#775;<sup>(n&minus;1)</sup> and determine which nozzles land the wall viscosity inside the 1-100 Pa&middot;s printability window.
2. **Thermal Gelation Kinetics:** Fit isothermal gelation to the Avrami law &alpha;(t) = 1 &minus; exp(&minus;k<sub>Av</sub>&middot;t<sup>m</sup>), locate the gel point (&alpha; = &frac12;), and track the storage modulus G&prime; building toward its plateau G&prime;<sub>&infin;</sub>.
3. **Cell Viability Through Shear:** Compute the wall shear stress &tau;<sub>wall</sub> = &eta;&middot;(4v/R) and the exponential survival S = exp(&minus;k<sub>kill</sub>&middot;&tau;<sub>wall</sub>&middot;t<sub>res</sub>), quantifying the resolution-viability trade-off against the 85% survival target.
4. **Scaffold Degradation & Lifetime:** Follow first-order hydrolytic mass loss M = M&#8320;e<sup>&minus;kt</sup> and the porous-strut stiffness scaling E &prop; M<sup>4/3</sup> to find the time a scaffold drops below its load-bearing floor E<sub>min</sub>.
5. **4D Tracheal-Stent Capstone:** Back-calculate a flat-printed bilayer that self-curls to a tracheal radius using Timoshenko curvature &kappa; = 6&Delta;&epsilon;(1+m)&sup2;/(h&middot;&phi;), with a 20% over-curvature margin and the deployment safety gates R<sub>f</sub> &gt; 95%, R<sub>r</sub> &gt; 90%, T<sub>g</sub> = 45-50 &deg;C.
