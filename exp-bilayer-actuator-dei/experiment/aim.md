### Aim of the Experiment
To design and quantify 4D-printed bilayer actuators that convert a thermal or moisture stimulus into programmed bending, twisting, and useful mechanical work across five coupled sub-calculations:
1. **Timoshenko Bimetal Bending:** Compute the equilibrium curvature (&kappa;), transformed-section neutral axis (y<sub>na</sub>), through-thickness bending stress &sigma;(y), and stored strain energy of a bilayer cantilever from the thickness ratio m = h<sub>1</sub>/h<sub>2</sub> and modulus ratio n = E<sub>1</sub>/E<sub>2</sub>.
2. **Hygroscopic Swelling:** Resolve the anisotropic swelling strains &epsilon;<sub>&parallel;</sub> = &beta;<sub>1</sub>&Delta;C and &epsilon;<sub>&perp;</sub> = &beta;<sub>2</sub>&Delta;C of a cellulose-fibre layer, rotate them by the fibre angle &theta;<sub>f</sub>, and separate the resulting bending from the twist driven by the shear strain &epsilon;<sub>xy</sub>.
3. **Print-Path Programming:** Map the FDM raster angles &theta;<sub>top</sub>/&theta;<sub>bot</sub> onto effective longitudinal and shear CTEs (&alpha;<sub>&parallel;</sub> = 10&times;10<sup>&minus;6</sup>/K, &alpha;<sub>&perp;</sub> = 100&times;10<sup>&minus;6</sup>/K) and predict whether the coupon bends, twists, or coils into a helix.
4. **Diffusion Timescale:** Apply Fickian transport (Fourier number &tau; = Dt/L<sup>2</sup>) to obtain the characteristic time t<sub>diff</sub> = L<sup>2</sup>/&pi;<sup>2</sup>D, the 90% response time t<sub>90</sub> &approx; 0.53 L<sup>2</sup>/D, and see why response time scales with thickness squared.
5. **Actuator Work Output:** Size a hygromorph by computing free-tip deflection, blocking force F<sub>block</sub>, mechanical work W = &frac12; F&delta;, and volumetric energy density, benchmarked against SMA and SMP actuators.
