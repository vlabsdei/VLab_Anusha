### Aim of the Experiment

**The big picture.** In 4D printing you print a flat or simple shape, and a stimulus - water, pH, or body heat - makes it transform over time into its final, working shape. This experiment teaches you to design a real 4D-printed **hydrogel** device from start to finish, by working through the five questions a 4D-printing engineer actually has to answer. Each sub-calculator is a real printed device, not just an equation:

1. **The self-folding gripper - "how much will it swell?" (Flory-Rehner).** A flat-printed hydrogel sheet curls up when it soaks up water. You compute the **swelling ratio Q** (how many times bigger it gets), which decides how tightly it folds - and therefore how big to print the flat pattern so it reaches the target shape.

2. **The pH-triggered drug capsule - "where will it open?" (Donnan / pH response).** A printed capsule must stay shut in stomach acid and pop open in the intestine. You compute the **pH at which it swells open**, so the drug is released in the right place in the body.

3. **The body-temperature valve - "how big is the snap?" (PNIPAM LCST).** A printed soft valve or muscle collapses sharply at ~32 C. You compute its **actuation stroke** and how **sharp** the on/off switch is.

4. **The cartilage scaffold - "is it as soft as the tissue?" (network modulus).** A printed tissue scaffold has to feel as stiff as the real tissue it replaces. You compute the **Young's modulus E** and check it lands in the cartilage/skin window.

5. **The drug-eluting implant - "how fast is the dose?" (Korsmeyer-Peppas).** A printed implant releases its drug over hours to days. You compute the **release profile and half-life** to set a safe dosing schedule.

**What you should be able to do at the end:** for each device, decide *what material, what print setting (crosslink density), and what stimulus* give the transformation you want - the core design loop of soft 4D printing.
