### Aim of the Experiment
To investigate and quantify non-thermal 4D actuation, where printed active structures are driven by light and magnetic fields rather than by bulk heating, across five distinct physical regimes:
1. **Photoswitchable Actuation:** Model the light-driven bending of an azobenzene-loaded strip using Beer-Lambert absorption and first-order photoisomerisation kinetics, and evaluate the actuation time t<sub>90</sub> and bend angle &theta; as functions of light intensity I<sub>0</sub> and dye loading C.
2. **Plasmonic Photothermal Triggering:** Characterise near-infrared (808&nbsp;nm) heating of a gold-nanorod implant, computing the steady temperature rise &Delta;T<sub>ss</sub>, the time-to-activation t<sub>act</sub>, and the confinement of the heated zone d for a chosen laser power P and nanorod loading.
3. **Magnetic Torque Steering:** Analyse the field-driven deflection of a ferromagnetically-loaded catheter tip through the magnetic torque &tau; = m &times; B, and determine the critical field B<sub>crit</sub> needed to steer a target angle as a function of particle volume fraction &phi;.
4. **Wireless Coil Powering:** Evaluate the on-axis field B of an external drive coil, the current I and power P required to reach B<sub>crit</sub> at implant depth r, and identify the crossover depth beyond which an on-board battery outperforms wireless coupling.
5. **Trigger Selection:** Compare the characteristic response times of magnetic (t<sub>mag</sub>), photo (t<sub>photo</sub>), and thermal (t<sub>therm</sub>) actuation, and map each stimulus to the application whose required response speed it best matches.
