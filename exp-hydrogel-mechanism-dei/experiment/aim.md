### Aim of the Experiment
To investigate and quantify how a printed hydrogel network converts a chemical or thermal stimulus into mechanical motion, across five design problems spanning swelling thermodynamics, ionic triggering, thermoresponse, tissue-matched stiffness, and controlled drug release:
1. **Flory-Rehner Swelling:** Solve the Flory-Rehner equilibrium for the polymer volume fraction v<sub>p</sub> and swelling ratio Q as a function of solvent affinity (&chi;) and crosslink density, then convert Q into the bilayer fold angle that closes a self-folding gripper.
2. **pH-Triggered Swelling:** Characterize weak-acid ionisation with Henderson-Hasselbalch (&alpha;) and the Donnan osmotic swelling it drives, predicting the pH window over which a drug capsule springs open in the gut.
3. **LCST Thermoresponse:** Model the sigmoidal collapse of a PNIPAM-type gel about its lower critical solution temperature (LCST &asymp; 32&deg;C), extracting the actuation stroke &epsilon;, volume change ratio, and switch sharpness (FWHM of dQ/dT) of a body-temperature valve.
4. **Swollen Rubber Elasticity:** Compute the dry shear modulus G = &nu;<sub>e</sub>k<sub>B</sub>T and its dilution on swelling (G<sub>sw</sub> = G<sub>dry</sub>Q<sup>-1/3</sup>, E = 3G), tuning crosslink density and swelling to land a scaffold inside cartilage's 0.1-1 MPa stiffness window.
5. **Korsmeyer-Peppas Release Kinetics:** Fit early-time drug release to the power law M<sub>t</sub>/M<sub>&infin;</sub> = kt<sup>n</sup>, reading the exponent n to classify the transport mechanism (Fickian &rarr; case-II) and estimating the half-dose time t<sub>50</sub> for a drug-eluting implant.
