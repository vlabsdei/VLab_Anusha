### Aim of the Experiment
To characterise the thermomechanical behaviour of Nitinol (NiTi) shape memory alloys using five standard laboratory instruments, extracting the transformation temperatures, mechanical response, electrical actuation, work output, and fatigue life that govern their use in 4D-printed active structures:
1. **Transformation Thermometry (DSC):** Measure the four transformation temperatures (M<sub>s</sub>, M<sub>f</sub>, A<sub>s</sub>, A<sub>f</sub>) from composition, track the martensite fraction x<sub>M</sub> through the cosine kinetics law, and quantify the thermal hysteresis A<sub>f</sub> &minus; M<sub>s</sub>.
2. **Mechanical Regimes (UTM):** Contrast the shape-memory-effect and superelastic stress-strain loops as a function of test temperature relative to A<sub>f</sub>, and compute the plateau stress &sigma;<sub>AM</sub>, dissipated hysteresis work, and damping capacity Q<sup>&minus;1</sup>.
3. **Electrical Actuation (Bench Supply & Thermocouple):** Joule-heat a Nitinol wire, solve the electro-thermal ODE for the temperature history, and find the steady-state temperature T<sub>ss</sub> and activation time t<sub>act</sub> to reach A<sub>f</sub>.
4. **Actuator Sizing (Load Cell & Ruler):** Size a wire actuator - contraction stroke d, blocking force F<sub>block</sub> = &sigma;<sub>recovery</sub> &times; A<sub>wire</sub>, and mass-specific work w<sub>sp</sub> - and benchmark it against competing actuator technologies.
5. **Fatigue Life (Rotating Bending):** Apply the Coffin-Manson relation N = C / &epsilon;<sup>2</sup> to predict structural fatigue life, and model the separate functional fatigue (decay of recoverable strain) over repeated cycling.
