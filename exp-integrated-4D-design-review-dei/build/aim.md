### Aim of the Experiment
To carry out an end-to-end engineering design review of a 4D-printed active structure, closing the loop from material choice to deployment readiness across five decision stages:
1. **Ashby Materials Selection:** Rank five 4D-active materials on three competing performance indices — bending stiffness M<sub>1</sub> = &radic;E/&rho;, recovery-stress per mass M<sub>2</sub> = &sigma;<sub>recovery</sub>/&rho;, and trigger efficiency M<sub>3</sub> = &eta;&middot;E<sub>stored</sub>/Q<sub>trigger</sub> — and show that the min&ndash;max-normalized weighted score M<sub>total</sub> re-ranks with the application.
2. **Transformation Verification:** Reconcile the design-intent curvature &kappa;<sub>pred</sub> = 1/R against the finite-strain &kappa;<sub>sim</sub> the folded bilayer actually produces, and gate the part on a shape error &epsilon;<sub>shape</sub> = |&kappa;<sub>sim</sub> &minus; &kappa;<sub>pred</sub>|/&kappa;<sub>pred</sub> &lt; 10 %.
3. **Lifecycle Energy Budget:** Sum print, programming, and per-cycle trigger energy of a 4D part over N actuations, E<sub>4D</sub>(N), against a moulded part plus powered actuator E<sub>conv</sub>(N), and locate the break-even N<sub>break</sub>.
4. **Manufacturing Scalability:** Compare flat 4D-print cost-per-part against injection moulding, whose fixed mould cost C<sub>mould</sub> amortizes over N parts, and find the production-volume crossover.
5. **Technology Readiness Assessment:** Place four 4D applications on the NASA/ISRO TRL 1&ndash;9 ladder, identify the barrier to the next rung, and trace each barrier back to a contradiction surfaced in an earlier sub-calc.
